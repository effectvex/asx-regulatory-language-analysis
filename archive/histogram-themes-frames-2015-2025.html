4. Histogram of regulatory-term incidence

This histogram shows how company-level six-term incidence is distributed in the selected year. Each bar counts companies whose annual report incidence falls within that interval; it complements the pooled annual line by showing whether a year-wide movement is broad-based or concentrated in a smaller tail of companies.

The selector redraws the distribution for each year from the same balanced panel. The orange curve is a fitted normal distribution using that year's mean (μ) and standard deviation (σ); dashed purple markers show ±1σ either side of the mean. The outlier button excludes companies whose incidence is more than 3σ from the mean and lists them below the chart.

This distribution is a useful guard against over-reading the aggregate line. If procedural words dominate the corpus, the index is primarily a measure of compliance vocabulary and governance disclosure, rather than an index of explicit regulatory frustration.

How themes change by year

The annual leaders table above reports the single most frequent theme. Because theme assignments overlap, a change in the leader does not mean other themes disappear. It identifies which organisational response is most visible in that year's coded passages and can highlight periods in which reporting, systems, climate, privacy or prudential language becomes more prominent.

In substantive terms, a recurring pairing of compliance/governance with systems/data indicates that regulation is being absorbed into operating infrastructure. A recurring cost/resourcing code alongside obligation/control indicates that companies acknowledge implementation work without necessarily presenting a separately quantified "regulatory cost" line.

Interpretation

Cost or challenge treats regulation as expense, complexity, delay, risk or resource pressure.

Obligation or control presents compliance as managed through policies, controls, monitoring, audits or mandated actions.

Transition or anticipation looks forward to proposed, evolving or newly effective rules and preparation work.

Opportunity or legitimacy links responsible compliance to trust, leadership, access or value creation.

Descriptive or neutral records a regulatory fact without a clear evaluative or forward-looking signal.

The most frequent frame is Obligation or control, accounting for 89,137 selected passages. This is consistent with a narrative in which companies operationalise regulation through governance routines and control systems rather than describing it as a standalone financial line item.
